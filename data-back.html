A digital companion to the installation

Land Back · Water Back · Data Back

One of the fastest-growing data-center corridors in the country runs the I-35 line from Austin to San Antonio — consuming the same water, power, and data the installation tries to give back. This is the before. The installation is the answer.

Scroll to grow the rhizome. Hover or tap any node for its source. Every number is attributed by name and shown as a projection or a range — none is a bare fact.

The surge

88 MW → 466.5 MW. Under-construction data-center capacity in the corridor rose roughly five-fold in a single year. — CBRE (H2 2023 → H2 2024)

Named projects cluster in Williamson and Caldwell counties: Tract's ~3,000-acre Caldwell Valley park (announced for 4+ GW), the Skybox/Prologis Hutto campus (~600 MW, ~$10B), and Blueprint Taylor, Blueprint Georgetown, and Sabey/SDC in Round Rock. — public filings; capacities announced, not built.

The demand

7,823 MW planned vs ~1,154 MW operating. Pipeline is not built capacity — and much may be the same load queued by several developers. — Cushman & Wakefield, 2026

ERCOT's large-load queue ran ~226 GW (Nov 2025, ~73% data centers) → ~410 GW (Mar 2026, ~87%). These are interconnection requests, not commitments. — ERCOT

ERCOT's preliminary forecast projects up to ~368,000 MW statewide by 2032 — preliminary and contested, against an all-time record peak of 85,508 MW. — ERCOT

The water

Texas data centers consume an estimated ~25 billion gallons/year today. — HARC, 2026

HARC projects 29–161 billion gal/yr by 2030 (up to ~2.7% of state water) — always the full range, never the headline alone. UT Austin projects 3–9% of Texas' water by 2040, up from under 1% now. — HARC; UT Austin (Jackson School)

The advocacy group GEAA estimates ~494,091 acre-feet by 2030 — an advocacy projection, not measured aquifer drawdown. No verified Edwards/Trinity site-level data exists yet. — GEAA

The gap — and Data Back

Facility-level water use is largely undisclosed — NDAs and aggregate-only reporting. We cannot answer what we cannot see. — Privette et al., AGU Advances, 2026

That gap is what Data Back answers. In the installation, your contribution is yours: Take it, Hear it, Gift it, or Erase it. Ephemeral by default, local-only, provenance-first. Nothing leaves the room; nothing is kept unless you gift it.

The three returns

Land Back. Water Back. Data Back. Three literal cycles of return, not a picture of one. The acceptance test for every technology in the work is the same one asked of the visitor: does it give back more than it takes?

Grounded in CARE and OCAP® data-sovereignty principles, and in research-as-ceremony — offering before taking. See the installation →
