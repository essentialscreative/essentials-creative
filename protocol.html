The protocol is named for sōsaku-hanga (創作版画) — the early-20th-century Japanese movement that asked every printmaker to own every step. The same demand falls on every contribution to our system: you propose what you have drawn, carved, or printed yourself, and the collective reviews together. No outsourcing the part where the work decides what it wants to be.

GOVERNED BY · CARE PRINCIPLES (GIDA, 2020) Indigenous-attributed motifs (Tenango · amate · maque · Sequoyah · Apache coil · Mexica calendrical glyphs · Tonkawa / Coahuiltecan forms) are governed by the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance: Collective benefit · Authority to control · Responsibility · Ethics. The protocol below operationalizes CARE for visual material. Sōsaku-hanga is the form; CARE is the ethical floor.

03 · THE FOUR STEPS · HOW TO CONTRIBUTE
iii · propose · carve · review · enter
iJi-ga · propose

Sketch the mark by hand.

The contributor draws the proposed motif (or composition, or amended discipline) on paper — graphite, ink, brush, whatever feels true. Hand first. Cite your own source: family piece, named artist, region, photographed object, oral teaching. If you cannot cite, do not propose.

Submit: the sketch (photo or scan), the citation, the heritage thread it belongs to, the proposed cell or slot in the system (see broadside).

iiJi-koku · carve

Translate to vector at fidelity.

Use the local tool at ~/Tools/sketch-to-svg/ for faithful raster→Bézier translation. Then carve by hand: add the negative-space cuts, fit the circle frame, refine the silhouette to kamon discipline. The tool does the grunt vectorization. The contributor holds the meaning.

Forbidden: AI-generated motifs for any Indigenous-attributed cell. Recraft / Vectorizer.AI / Galileo are permitted only for abstract patterns and composition stubs — never for Otomí · Purépecha · Apache · Cherokee · Tejano · Tonkawa · Coahuiltecan · Comanche · Lipan Apache forms. See Panel 05.

iiiAizuri · review

The collective reviews together.

The contribution circulates to all members for review. Two-Row test: does the new mark stay distinct, in clear relation to other lineage rows, without flattening them? Iki test: is anything being unsaid that would be richer if held back? Notan test: does it pass dark/light at 2cm? Cite-test: is the source named, locatable, consented?

Indigenous-attributed contributions require additional consent: a member of that lineage (Otomí, Purépecha, etc.) must sign off. If no such member exists in the collective, the mark stays as a HOLD placeholder until relationship is established.

ivJi-zuri · enter

The motif enters the library — attributed, licensed, dated.

On approval, the SVG enters the motif library panel with: contributor name, lineage citation, license badge (see Panel 04), date entered, edition mark for its first appearance. The corresponding SLOT or DRAFT/HOLD placeholder is removed and the absence becomes presence.

The motif is now part of the system. Future drops may use it freely under its license. Retirements are also recorded — every entry has a date in, and may someday have a date out, written into the decisions archive.

04 · LICENSE · WHAT EACH MARK CARRIES
iv · four shapes · pick the right one
DEFAULT · CC BY-NC-SA 4.0

Most marks.

Attribution required (contributor + EC). Non-commercial. ShareAlike (derivatives carry the same license). Anyone may reuse on a flyer, a zine, a movement print — as long as the attribution stays and the license travels.

This is the spine of the library. Justseeds-shaped: maximum movement use, maximum credit.

APPLIES TO · system-original marks · abstract patterns · Tejas-native plant/animal silhouettes · geometric primitives
RESTRICTED · CC BY-NC-ND 4.0

Member family pieces.

Attribution + non-commercial + no derivatives. Used as-is, never modified. For marks tied to a specific family or personal lineage (e.g., a member's family mon, a contributor's own published artwork).

Contributor retains right to revoke at any time. EC keeps no claim beyond the agreed use.

APPLIES TO · Japanese family mon · member-authored work · cited personal lineage pieces
CULTURALLY RESTRICTED · CARE

Indigenous-attributed motifs.

Not under Creative Commons. Governed by CARE Principles and by the originating community's own protocols (Otomí refuse stock-library use of Tenango / amate spirits; Apache and Cherokee per nation-specific consent).

Use only under named permission from a member of the lineage, with consent renewable per project. Never released to general public reuse. Cite first, ask second, use third.

APPLIES TO · Tenango · amate cuts · maque · Sequoyah · Apache coil · Mexica calendrical glyphs
SACRED / TRADITIONAL · DO NOT REPRODUCE

Sacred forms.

Some forms are not for the library. Ceremonial regalia, dance-mask elements, sacred-bundle imagery, certain Mesoamerican glyphs of named deities — these are recognized as outside the system entirely. Naming the absence is the contribution.

If a member proposes a sacred mark, the protocol's first answer is "no." The discussion that follows may produce a related but non-sacred mark instead.

05 · THE BRIGHT LINE · AI + INDIGENOUS MOTIFS
v · what AI can do · what it cannot

AI is grunt vectorization, never authorship.

The 2026 AI tools (Recraft V4, Vectorizer.AI, Adobe Firefly, Galileo, Figma AI) produce real SVG output now. Many are useful. None are permitted to author Indigenous-attributed motifs in this system.

This is not a stylistic preference. It is governance under CARE Authority-to-Control: the right of Indigenous peoples to control how data and visual material from their nations is created, used, and circulated. An AI trained on scraped Tenango embroideries or amate cuts cannot consent to its own training; the nation cannot consent to its likeness becoming a stylistic option in a tool. The line is bright because it has to be.

ALLOWED · AI AS TOOL Sketch-to-SVG vectorization of a hand-drawn contributor mark. Composition stubs (suggest layouts, scale figures, generate halftone fields). Abstract pattern fills. Font discovery. Color-pair contrast checking. Background removal. Always with a human at the carve step.
FORBIDDEN · AI AS AUTHOR Generating Otomí · Purépecha · Apache · Cherokee · Tejano · Tonkawa · Coahuiltecan · Comanche · Lipan Apache · Mexica · Maya · or any Indigenous-attributed motif from a text prompt. Generating "in the style of" any Indigenous tradition. Generating sacred or ceremonial forms from any tradition, ever.

What every accepted contribution carries.

Every entry in the library carries: contributor name (or "EC collective" if collectively authored); lineage citation (tradition + region + source); license badge (one of the four shapes in Panel 04); date entered (YYYY-MM-DD); first-edition mark (which drop the motif debuted in).

The protocol is reviewed each solstice and equinox — four times a year. The collective gathers, walks the library, considers proposed entries, considers retirements, considers amendments. Decisions are recorded in decisions/ as ADRs.

Revisions: this document is v0.1. Member feedback amends it. Major revisions bump the version. The full edit history lives in git (or in the printed CHANGELOG insert each riso run).
