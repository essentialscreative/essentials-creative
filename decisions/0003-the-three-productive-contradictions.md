# ADR-0003 · Keep the three productive contradictions alive

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-05-27
- **Supersedes:** —
- **Superseded by:** —

## Context

The three movements we draw from contradict each other in real, observable ways. Most "Japanese minimalism" treatments flatten these into one mood. The flattening loses the engine.

## Decision

**Name the three contradictions and keep them alive** as the design questions of the system — not bugs to resolve.

### α · Sōsaku-hanga vs. Mingei

- *Sōsaku-hanga:* the artist owns every step and signs the work.
- *Mingei:* the craftsman is anonymous and serves utility.
- **Our resolution (after Serizawa Keisuke):** Essentials Creative is the **named anonymous** — the collective signs every output; individual members disappear into the collective's signature.

### β · Mingei vs. Hisamatsu (Zen aesthetic)

- *Mingei:* beauty in everyday utility (the rice bowl used at every meal).
- *Zen aesthetics:* originally elite, monastic, tea-ceremony (the rice bowl used once at a tea gathering).
- **Our resolution:** *two registers, same grammar.* Everyday work = Mingei (IG drops, planting flyers, zines hand-to-hand). Ceremonial work = Zen (installations, the FAE deck, one-off cut paper).

### γ · Onchi expression vs. Seijaku calm

- *Onchi:* print as expression — emotional, dynamic, individual.
- *Seijaku (Hisamatsu):* energized calm — stillness with tension.
- **Our resolution:** *pressure inside calm.* The subject is urgent (Land Back · Water Back · the monarch refusing the border). The medium is still. Mori Yoshitoshi's prints embody this — dynamic subjects forced flat and still by the stencil.

## Consequences

- A piece that *resolves* one of these contradictions has betrayed the grammar.
- Tone of the work is "pressure inside calm" — never glib, never decorative-only.
- Member-attribution discipline (resolution α) is structural, not stylistic — solves the Hanga Protocol licensing question simultaneously.
